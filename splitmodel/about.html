What this page is

These are the same games as the main model, priced from a different reading of recent form. Everything downstream of the rates - the regression, log5, the base-out simulator, advancement, park and umpire handling - is shared code. Only the plate appearances feeding the rates change.

Windows are games, not days

The main model looks back a fixed number of days. A calendar window hands every player the same number of days and a wildly different number of plate appearances: over seven days a regular gets around thirty, a platoon bat gets nine, a starter gets one outing or two depending on where his turn fell, and a player who sat with a tight hamstring gets zero and quietly reverts to league average. These windows are the last 15, 7 and 3 games played, which equalises the sample the regression actually cares about.

Each player is read from today's side

A player starting on the road is rated from his road games only, and a player at home from his home games. That reaches back further in calendar time than it sounds: teams travel in series, so a hitter's last three road games are usually four to eight days back, and his last fifteen are most of six weeks. For a starting pitcher, who works every fifth day and splits his turns between home and road, fifteen road starts is close to a full season.

Park factors are removed before they are applied

A home split is built entirely from one park, so the observed rates already carry it. Applying the park factor again on top would count it twice, which at the extreme parks is worth real runs. Every rate here is divided by the plate-appearance-weighted park factor of the games it was built from before the simulator applies today's park. The correction acts on the observed counts alone, not on the blend, because the regression anchor is already park-neutral.

Anchors are season-long on purpose

The target a short window is regressed toward is always built from season-to-date data. Anchoring a twelve-plate-appearance sample to a twelve-plate-appearance estimate of where it should be anchored is not regression; it is noise regressed toward itself.

These numbers are not backtested

The calibration constants and the backtest figures on the main site were both measured on season rates. Neither carries over to these windows. Expect the short windows to show less spread between games than the season column, not more: every rate is regressed by sample size, so a three-game window hands most of the weight to the anchor and the teams converge. A form column wider than the season column is a symptom of a problem, not a sign of sharper reads.

Model output, not advice. Held-out error is about 3.7 runs a game and the model does not yet beat a league-average constant — treat every number as a wide estimate.
